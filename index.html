The Problem

Most prescriptions are filled with generics. Every one needs a filing, and much of each filing is careful, repetitive writing.

9 in 10

US prescriptions are filled with generics

600

new ANDAs filed with FDA in fiscal 2025

Each with its own labeling and regional documents.

30–60

pages in a typical prescribing label

Every word has to be accounted for in the generic.

…the same as the labeling of the reference listed drug except for differences annotated and explained 21 CFR 314.94(a)(8)

Word for word, by hand

Teams copy the reference label, change what the law allows, and write a reason for every difference, mostly in Word.

One miss can cost months

A protected use left in, or a cross-reference pointing at removed text, can draw an FDA deficiency and delay approval.

It never stays done

When the brand label changes, every generic that follows it has to be updated and re-annotated.

Our Solution

Software does the volume. People keep the judgment.

  1. Research

    Regulark gathers what the section depends on: the reference label, Orange Book patents and exclusivities, and FDA's product-specific guidance.

  2. Draft

    It writes the section in FDA's vocabulary, and traces every sentence to its source.

  3. Annotate

    Each difference from the reference drug is marked and numbered, with its reason, the way the annotated comparison is laid out.

  4. Approve and record

    A named person on your team approves each change. Every decision goes into a record that shows if it is ever altered.

Your Whole ANDA,
Starting with Module 1

We start with US FDA filings, where the rules are most exact, then move through the rest of the dossier and into other markets.

Module 1, where we start. The US regional section: cover letter, basis for submission, the bioavailability waiver request, and the labeling, including the annotated comparison with the reference drug.

Module 2. Summaries of the information in Modules 3 to 5, written to a fixed structure.

Module 3. Quality: the drug substance, the finished product, and how they are made and tested.

Module 4. Nonclinical study reports, where a filing needs them.

Module 5. Clinical study reports. For a generic, mainly the bioequivalence studies showing it matches the reference drug.
